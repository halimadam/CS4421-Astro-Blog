Pool is easier to enjoy when everyone at the table knows what is happening and has room to play. Most good etiquette is simple: communicate clearly, respect the other player's turn, and take care of the shared space. House rules and venue customs differ, so a quick question is better than assuming everyone plays the same way.

## Agree on the game first

Before the first shot, confirm which game you are playing and how you will handle details that might come up. In 8-ball, that can include how groups are assigned, what counts as a legal break, and what happens if the 8-ball is pocketed on the break. Different leagues and casual groups may use different rules.

If a rule is unclear during play, pause briefly and settle it calmly before the next shot. When possible, consult the rules everyone agreed to use. Treating a disagreement as a shared question usually keeps a small uncertainty from taking over the game.

## Give the shooter room

Stay out of the shooter's line of sight and avoid standing close to the shot. Do not move around, reach across the table, or talk while someone is lining up. Wait until the stroke is complete before approaching the table or collecting balls.

Keep cues, drinks, and personal items off the playing surface. Leaning on the table can disturb the cloth or interfere with a shot, and placing a drink where it can spill risks damaging the table. If you need to move around, do so when it will not distract or obstruct the shooter.

## Keep turns clear

Pay attention to the game and make it obvious whose turn it is. If a ball's status or the result of a shot is uncertain, ask rather than moving balls or assuming the turn is over. When you are not shooting, give the player space to decide and avoid offering advice unless they ask for it.

Play at a considerate pace. Take enough time to choose a shot, but avoid long delays between turns when the table is shared. If you need to step away, let the other player know and agree on how to pause.

## Look after the shared table

Use the house equipment respectfully, return cues and balls where they belong, and follow the venue's guidance about chalk and table care. Do not mark the cloth or use equipment in ways it was not intended to be used. If something is damaged or a ball gets stuck, tell the staff instead of trying a risky repair.

Courtesy does not need to be formal. A clear question, a little space, and attention to the other player's turn make a casual game more comfortable for everyone.
