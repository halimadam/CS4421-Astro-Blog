A repeatable shot starts before the cue reaches the cue ball. Your stance gives your body balance, and your bridge gives the cue a steady track. Neither needs to look exactly like another player's; it needs to feel comfortable, stable, and consistent enough that you can aim and stroke without fighting your setup.

## Find a balanced stance

Stand behind the shot first and look along the intended line. When you step in, place your front foot near that line and set the other foot far enough away to support your weight. The exact foot position depends on which hand you shoot with and what feels natural. Aim for a relaxed base rather than a rigid pose.

Bend from the hips until your eyes can see the cue line clearly. Keep your head still while you make practice strokes, and let your back arm hang comfortably so the forearm can swing. If you feel off balance or have to twist your shoulder to reach the shot, stand up and reset instead of trying to rescue the position mid-stroke.

## Build a useful bridge

For an open bridge, plant your hand on the cloth and spread your fingers. Raise the knuckle of your index finger against your thumb to make a channel for the cue. The cue should slide smoothly without the bridge hand shifting as you stroke.

A closed bridge wraps the index finger over the cue and can feel more secure for some shots. Both forms can work. Choose the one that lets you guide the cue straight and maintain a comfortable grip. On a rail or near an obstructing ball, you may need a rail bridge or another adapted hand position; keep the cue supported and avoid forcing your wrist into an uncomfortable angle.

## Check the cue line

Once your bridge is planted, make a few slow practice strokes. The cue should move through the bridge in a straight path, and your grip should stay loose enough for a smooth swing. Watch whether the tip returns to the same address point each time. If it drifts sideways, adjust your stance or bridge before taking the shot.

For a simple stop shot, aim through the center of the cue ball and keep the cue as level as the table allows. Follow through along the line rather than pulling up at contact. On other shots the cue-ball contact point changes, but a steady bridge and a straight delivery still help you reach it accurately.

## Make the routine repeatable

Take your time while choosing the line, then settle into the stance without rushing. After the shot, notice whether your body stayed balanced and the cue followed through freely. Small, deliberate adjustments are easier to learn from than changing your feet, grip, and bridge all at once.

There is no single perfect stance for every body or every table. A good setup is one you can repeat, see clearly from, and deliver the cue through without unnecessary tension.
